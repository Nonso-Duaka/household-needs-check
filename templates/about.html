About this tool

What it is

A teaching demonstration. It takes a short household form and returns three explainable outputs: an estimated food-insecurity risk, a transparent hardship score, and a suggested policy-priority segment. It exists to show students how a screening model is built, how it can be read, and how easily it can be fooled by leakage.

What it is not

It is not an eligibility determination, not a benefits decision, and not a substitute for a caseworker. The data is fully synthetic and intentionally oversampled for hardship — it is not calibrated to real prevalence and must never be used to make claims about real people, places, or programs. Outputs are always phrased as "estimated," "suggested for review," or a "recommended starting point," never "eligible," "denied," or "qualifies."

Your data is not stored

The app is stateless. Answers are used to compute a result and are never persisted, logged, or sent anywhere. Reload the page and it is gone.

Fairness: what we deliberately do not ask

The form does not collect race or ethnicity, immigrant status, primary language, or disability status. These are treated as audit dimensions only: the model is checked offline for disparate error rates across them, but the app never collects them and never conditions a prediction on them. Excluding them from the form is a design choice, not an oversight.

Three fields are computed, not asked

Housing-cost burden, income-to-poverty ratio, and a food-desert flag are derived on the server from your answers. The exact rules — including two that had to be reverse-engineered from the data and how confident we are in each — are documented in notes/derived.md.
