Why a field defined "after hardship effects" cannot be used

Two columns in the dataset look like fantastic predictors and are quietly poisonous:

  • food_budget_monthly_usd — the data dictionary defines it as the budget remaining "after income, housing cost, benefits, and hardship effects." It is computed downstream of the same hardship process that produces the label.
  • mental_health_stress_score — defined as a score "correlated with household hardship."

A model using them is partly reading its own answer key. It looks brilliant in validation and would be useless — or misleading — in the field, because a household filling out a form cannot answer either one. Dropping them takes ROC AUC from 0.89 down to about 0.75. That drop is not a defect. It is the difference between a number that flatters the model and a number you could actually stand behind.

The honest model is the weaker-looking one. A screening tool is only as good as the questions a real person can answer. We ship the ~0.75 model on purpose.
